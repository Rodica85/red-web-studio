Google Isn't Going Anywhere, But AI Search Is Growing Fast

You'll hear a lot of noise right now about search engines being "replaced" by AI within a couple of years. That's an exaggeration: Google still handles the overwhelming majority of commercial search traffic, and that isn't changing overnight. What is true is that AI-powered search and chat assistants are growing quickly as a discovery channel, and they work differently to a traditional Google result. They read structured data, clearly written facts, and machine-readable files like llms.txt to decide what to cite and recommend.

The sensible strategy isn't picking one over the other. It's doing both properly. Every site we build is optimised for classic Google SEO and for AI-search visibility at the same time, so you're not caught out however your future customers decide to search.

Local Search Is Where Most Independent Businesses Win

If you serve customers in a specific area (a restaurant, a salon, a tradesperson, a clinic), local search is usually where the real value is, not generic national rankings. That means a properly optimised Google Business Profile, consistent name/address/phone details across the web, local schema markup so Google understands exactly where you operate, and content that naturally mentions the areas you serve. Get this right and you start showing up in the map pack and "near me" searches, which is exactly where someone ready to book today is looking.

If your business operates entirely online with no fixed location, we shift the emphasis instead: stronger topical content, clearer service pages like this one, and structured data that describes what you do rather than where you are. The SEO setup is tailored to how your customers actually find businesses like yours, not a one-size-fits-all checklist.

Why This Matters More For Independent Businesses

A large chain can afford to be found through brand recognition and paid ads. An independent restaurant, salon, or solicitor's practice usually can't compete on ad spend, so organic search is often the cheapest, most sustainable way new customers find you. Getting the SEO foundations right at launch, rather than trying to retrofit them onto a site later, is the difference between a website that quietly builds momentum over months and one that never gets discovered at all.
